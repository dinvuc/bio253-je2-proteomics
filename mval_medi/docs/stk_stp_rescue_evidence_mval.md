# Stk1/Stp1 rescue and suppression: evidence for the selected project

Literature checked 5 November 2026. The local project measurements have not yet been analysed.

## Distinguish three meanings

- **Complementation:** put the disrupted gene back and assess restoration of the measured phenotype.
- **Genetic suppression:** disrupting a second gene reduces or changes the first disruption's effect. This need not restore the wild-type state.
- **Compensation:** an alternative biological process counteracts a disruption. Remaining phosphorylation alone does not establish compensation.

## 1. Kinase loss can suppress aspects of phosphatase loss under acid stress

Huemer et al. (2023), Science Signaling 16, eabj8194, Fig. 3F-H and accompanying text: in Cowan I exposed to pH 5.5 for three days, the pknB/stp double deletion resembled the kinase deletion for small-colony formation, ATP and protein synthesis. This supports phenotype-specific suppression, not universal restoration. These conditions differ from our JE2 supernatant experiment.

Source: https://doi.org/10.1126/scisignal.abj8194 ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13625852/ . The course-provided PDF was also checked, printed pages 5-6. PknB is the kinase also called Stk1.

## 2. A closely relevant polar insertion has already been reported

Sun et al. (2012), PNAS 109, 15461-15466, Fig. 2E and Fig. S3: a bursa aurealis insertion in stp1 was reported to inactivate both stp1 and downstream stk1. Extracts from this strain showed reduced phosphorylation of the tested regulators, unlike a clean stp1 deletion. This is precedent in a different strain/assay, not verification of our insertion.

Source: https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1205952109 ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3458358/ . The phosphorylation assays concern SarA/MgrA-family proteins and reported cysteine phosphorylation. Our STY/STYH searches do not test cysteine phosphorylation, so this paper supports the genetic-interpretation issue rather than providing directly measurable cysteine-site targets for our analysis.

## 3. Adding the missing gene back can restore specific phenotypes

Burnside et al. (2010), PLOS ONE 5, e11071, Fig. 3 and the complementation results: expression of the respective missing gene restored alpha-toxin expression and hemolytic activity towards wild-type levels in Newman stp1 and stk1 deletion strains. This is add-back rescue, not evidence that one deletion rescues the other.

Source: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0011071 ; https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0011071 .

## 4. Losing both enzymes does not rescue everything

Beltramini et al. (2009), Infection and Immunity 77, 1406-1416: the N315 double mutant had prominent cell-division abnormalities and altered antibiotic susceptibility. Its phenotype was not a general return to wild type. The authors specifically checked that their single-deletion constructs did not have the same polar effect.

Source: https://doi.org/10.1128/IAI.01499-08 ; https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/iai.01499-08 .

## Implication for our project

A plausible interpretation to test is that reduced kinase in stp masks or suppresses some consequences expected from phosphatase loss alone. Another is that kinase-loss effects dominate certain pathways while other changes remain distinct. Neither explanation is a project result yet.

Prioritize measured enzyme amounts and genotype-by-condition interactions. Do not call similar interaction effects proof of rescue; similar mutants can both be abnormal. Do not treat the stp insertion as equivalent to a clean, complete double knockout.

To establish causality, useful follow-up comparisons would include parental, clean stp-only deletion retaining Stk1, clean stk-only deletion, verified double deletion, and appropriate complemented strains. These are proposed experiments, not strains available in our current dataset.

## Evidence limits

This targeted search establishes examples, not an exhaustive catalogue. The acid-stress results are not evidence that the same suppression occurs under PAO1 spent-medium exposure. The polar-insertion precedent is not the same genetic background or verified insertion as our JE2 course strains. Proteomics alone cannot distinguish transcriptional polarity from protein stability, regulatory feedback or an additional mutation.
