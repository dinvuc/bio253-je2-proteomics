# Abstract (draft for the A0 poster)

**Title:** Does the *stp1* transposon mutant behave like a kinase mutant? Proteomics of
*Staphylococcus aureus* JE2 Stk1/Stp1 mutants exposed to *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* supernatant

**Authors:** group mval (Bio253, HS 2026)

*Staphylococcus aureus* senses its environment partly through the serine/threonine kinase
Stk1 (PknB) and the phosphatase Stp1, encoded next to each other in one operon. We asked
whether the *stp1*::Tn mutant (NE1919) also lacks Stk1, and whether either mutant responds
differently from the parent JE2 to cell-free spent medium of *P. aeruginosa* PAO1 (PASN).
We analysed total proteomes (2,232 proteins) and phosphoproteomes of JE2, *stk1*::Tn and
*stp1*::Tn grown in TSB or with 30 % PASN (n = 3), using limma with a fold-change
threshold test (|log2FC| > 1, BH-FDR < 0.05), observed values only.
Stk1 was not detected in the *stk1* mutant in TSB or PASN and was ~43-fold lower (log2 −5.4) in the
*stp1* mutant, which retained only 1–2 Stk1 peptides (29–32 in JE2). Stp1 dropped to
0–2 peptides (9–15 in JE2). The transposon marker ErmB was found in all 18 mutant samples
and in none of 9 JE2 samples. Both mutants kept most of the PASN response (r = 0.85 and
0.86 vs JE2); only 5 (*stk1*) and 1 (*stp1*) of ~1,900 tested proteins changed their
response. Beyond shared noise, the two mutants' effects correlated moderately
(r = 0.37–0.38). Phosphosite coverage was sparse (236 usable sites); the Stk1 phosphosite
fell with the Stk1 protein. Two contiguous genomic blocks (a SigB/*mazEF*/*kdp* region in
*stk1*, a prophage in *stp1*) changed together and need DNA-level checks.
The *stp1*::Tn strain is therefore effectively a phosphatase mutant with strongly reduced
kinase, and must not be read as a phosphatase-only mutant.
